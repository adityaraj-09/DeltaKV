\begin{abstract}
Large-language-model serving systems reuse key--value (KV) states across
requests that share a token prefix, but they treat a \emph{weight} update
as a full cache miss.
vLLM namespaces prefix blocks by adapter identity, SGLang isolates by
weight version, and reinforcement-learning trainers flush KV on every
policy step.
The result is that the most expensive tensor in the stack is discarded
whenever $\Delta W$ is nonzero, even when that $\Delta W$ is small by
construction---a rank-$r$ LoRA, a rank-1 edit, a quantization-grid change,
or a KL-bounded RL step.
We observe that the KV cache is a materialized view of
$(\mathrm{weights}\times\mathrm{tokens})$ and that a weight update is
therefore an instance of incremental view maintenance.
\dkv\ is a library that, given a compact $\Delta W$ descriptor, produces
the corresponding $\Delta$KV without a full prefill.
Cache keys are token prefixes only; adapter identity and weight version
are edges of a lineage graph, not part of the key.
Where input activations are stored, the projection
$\Delta K=\mathrm{RoPE}(X\Delta W_K^\top)$, $\Delta V=X\Delta W_V^\top$
is algebraically exact because RoPE is linear.
Deeper layers are maintained by a hybrid of the adapter path on cached
hidden states, rank-$r$ least-squares onto the LoRA column space,
first-order Jacobian propagation of $\Delta X$ with snapshot resets, and
a spectral-condition-anchored probe budget.
A compatibility lattice $\mathrm{strict}\to\mathrm{patched}\text{-}\varepsilon\to\mathrm{miss}$
guarantees that the system is pointwise never worse than
flush-and-recompute.
On a Llama-style reference Transformer, a rank-4 LoRA of scale $10^{-2}$
is patched with next-token KL of $0$~nats at reported precision, relative
KV $\ell_2$ of $2.1\times 10^{-3}$ (inside a 4-bit KIVI noise floor), and
$8.5\%$ of re-prefill FLOPs.
\dkv\ does not fork vLLM or SGLang; it implements the connector
interfaces those engines already expose.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Prefix caching is the dominant source of reuse in LLM serving.
PagedAttention~\citep{kwon2023vllm} and radix-tree matching~\citep{zheng2024sglang}
share KV tensors across requests with a common token prefix.
That reuse has two well-studied axes: the \emph{token} prefix and the
\emph{position} encoding~\citep{su2024rope}.
A third axis is almost always ignored: the \emph{weights}.

Whenever the weights change---loading or swapping a
LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora,dettmers2023qlora}, applying a rank-1
edit~\citep{meng2022rome,meng2023memit}, changing a quantization grid, or
taking a policy-gradient step---production engines invalidate the entire
KV store.
vLLM hashes the adapter name into the prefix-block key and, on a
same-name reload, can reuse stale blocks~\citep{vllm-lora-stale,vllm-rl-rfc}.
SGLang places a weight version in the radix extra
key~\citep{sglang-weight-version} and flushes persistent cache tiers.
Distributed RL stacks reset every KV byte on every policy step.
In all of these settings $\Delta W$ is \emph{structurally small}: rank
$r\ll d$, rank $1$, or KL-bounded.
Throwing away $O(Lnd_{\mathrm{kv}})$ cached state is then an
implementation choice, not a mathematical necessity.

We treat KV as a materialized view over $(\mathrm{weights}\times\mathrm{tokens})$
and a weight update as incremental view
maintenance~\citep{gupta1993ivm}.
\dkv\ is the corresponding library.
A weight update ships a compact \emph{delta descriptor}.
The next lookup of a cached prefix materializes a patched view.
If a conservative error estimate exceeds a budget $\varepsilon$, the
engine falls back to a full prefill---exactly today's behavior.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item A cache contract in which keys are token prefixes only and
compatibility is the lattice
$\mathrm{strict}\to\mathrm{patched}\text{-}\varepsilon\to\mathrm{miss}$
(\S\ref{sec:contract}, Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).
\item Exact projection patches.
Because RoPE is linear,
$\mathrm{RoPE}(K+\Delta K)=\mathrm{RoPE}(K)+\mathrm{RoPE}(\Delta K)$.
At any layer whose input $X$ is stored, $\Delta K$ and $\Delta V$ are
closed form (\S\ref{sec:exact}).
\item A rank-$r$ LoRA-subspace corrector: per-token least-squares onto
$\mathrm{col}(B)$, an orthogonal residual shift, and a residual
gate~\citep{yao2024cacheblend} (\S\ref{sec:subspace}).
\item Spectral-condition hybrid routing---per-layer skip / exact /
subspace / probe---together with first-order $\Delta X$ propagation and
periodic snapshot resets (\S\ref{sec:hybrid}--\S\ref{sec:analytic}).
\item Connectors, not forks: vLLM \texttt{KVConnectorBase\_V1}, SGLang
weight-update hooks, LMCache hidden-state snapshots, and PEFT
state-dict extraction (\S\ref{sec:systems}).
\item A reference validator on a Llama-style stack (RMSNorm, RoPE, GQA,
SwiGLU) that reports next-token KL, log-probability $L_1$, and a
KIVI-4bit floor~\citep{liu2024kivi}, not KV-$\ell_2$ alone
(\S\ref{sec:exp}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Cache contract}
\label{sec:contract}

A decoder-only Transformer~\citep{touvron2023llama} maps a token prefix
$x_{1:n}$ and weights $W$ to a KV cache
$C=\mathrm{KV}(W,x_{1:n})\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times n\times n_{\mathrm{kv}}\times d_h}$
and a next-token distribution $\pi(\cdot\mid x_{1:n};W,C)$.
A \emph{weight version} $v$ is a node in a lineage DAG.
A \emph{weight delta} $\Delta W_{v\to v'}$ is a compact descriptor of
$W_{v'}-W_v$ (\S\ref{sec:delta}).
Given $C$ computed under $v$, the serving problem is to produce a cache
that is a valid view under $v'$ without a full prefill.

\paragraph{Entry.}
A cache row is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{CacheEntry}
  \;=\;
  \bigl(C_{\mathrm{base}},\;(\Delta C_i)_i,\;\hat\varepsilon\bigr),
\end{equation}
the base KV under $v_0$, a chain of applied patches, and a conservative
error estimate.
The physical key is $\mathrm{hash}(x_{1:n})$.
Adapter name and weight version label edges of the lineage graph; they
are not part of the key.

\paragraph{Lookup.}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{lookup}(x_{1:n},W_{\mathrm{cur}})
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \mathrm{STRICT}  & \text{if } v(C)=v_{\mathrm{cur}},\\
    \mathrm{PATCHED} & \text{if a }\Delta W\text{ path exists and }\hat\varepsilon\le\varepsilon,\\
    \mathrm{MISS}    & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
A miss is flush-and-recompute.
A patch whose \emph{measured} residual exceeds $\varepsilon$ is promoted
to a miss.
The engine is therefore pointwise never less correct than today's
invalidation policy (Figure~\ref{alg:lookup}).

\textit{Input:} prefix $x_{1:n}$, current version $v_{\mathrm{cur}}$, budget $\varepsilon$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=4pt]
\item $e \gets \mathrm{store}[\mathrm{hash}(x_{1:n})]$. If $e$ is nil, prefill and insert.
\item If $e.v = v_{\mathrm{cur}}$, return \textsc{strict}: replay $e.\mathrm{base}+\sum e.\mathrm{patches}$.
\item If there is no $\Delta W$ path $e.v\to v_{\mathrm{cur}}$, or $\|\Delta W\|$ is outside the patchable regime, return \textsc{miss}: prefill.
\item Pick a route whose billed FLOPs are strictly below prefill (default: hybrid).
\item $C' \gets \mathrm{materialize}(e,\Delta W)$. If the residual is measured and $\hat\varepsilon>\varepsilon$, return \textsc{miss}: prefill.
\item Append the full increment $C'-e.\mathrm{base}$; rebase if $\hat\varepsilon\ge 0.8\,\varepsilon$. Return \textsc{patched}: $C'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}

\label{alg:lookup}

\paragraph{Acceptance criteria.}
A patched cache is acceptable when
(i)~$\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\mathrm{fresh}}\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{patched}})\lesssim 10^{-2}$~nats,
(ii)~max-layer relative KV $\ell_2\lesssim 0.10$, and
(iii)~that $\ell_2$ lies inside a 4-bit grouped-quantization noise
floor~\citep{liu2024kivi}.
Serving samples from $\pi$; off-policy RL depends on
$|\log\pi_{\mathrm{fresh}}-\log\pi_{\mathrm{patched}}|$.
Relative KV $\ell_2$ alone is not a sufficient metric.

The \emph{patchable regime} is the set of $\Delta W$ that are small by
construction.
If a Frobenius proxy exceeds a threshold (default $0.5$), \dkv\ refuses
to patch: full supervised fine-tuning and architecture changes are a
miss.

\section{Exact projection patches}
\label{sec:exact}

Let $X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$ be the RMS-normalized input of a
$k$/$v$ projection.
Then
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:exact}
  \Delta K \;=\; \mathrm{RoPE}\!\bigl(X\Delta W_K^\top\bigr),\qquad
  \Delta V \;=\; X\Delta W_V^\top.
\end{equation}
RoPE is a block-diagonal linear operator on each adjacent pair of
dimensions, so
$\mathrm{RoPE}(K+\Delta K)=\mathrm{RoPE}(K)+\mathrm{RoPE}(\Delta K)$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:exact} is algebraically exact at any layer whose $X$
is the true input under the new weights: layer~$0$ when embeddings are
frozen, and every layer whose residual-stream snapshot is stored.
For LoRA this is the adapter path, $O(ndr)$ against $O(nd^2)$ for the
dense map.
Storage is $nr+rd$ codes and basis until RoPE mixes the $K$ basis, after
which a dense increment of size $n\,d_{\mathrm{kv}}$ is kept---still one
layer, still not a re-prefill.

Above the first modified layer, $X_\ell$ depends on $W$, so
\eqref{eq:exact} on a stale $X_\ell$ is a zeroth-order approximation and
the baseline that the routes in \S\ref{sec:deep} correct.

\section{Deep-layer routes}
\label{sec:deep}

\subsection{Rank-\texorpdfstring{$r$}{r} subspace correction}
\label{sec:subspace}

A rank-1 probe corrector estimates a chunk offset $\hat\mu$ from a few
recomputed tokens and adds it to the rest.
Production LoRA is rank-$r$: $\Delta V$ lives in $\mathrm{col}(B)$.
Let $R=C_{\mathrm{fresh}}-C_{\mathrm{reused}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_{\mathrm{kv}}}$
be a residual estimate (exact on probes; mixed-forward otherwise) and
$B\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{kv}}\times r}$ the LoRA factor of
$v_{\mathrm{proj}}$.
Per token,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ls}
  c_j
  \;=\;
  (B^\top B+\lambda I)^{-1} B^\top R_{j,:}^\top,
\end{equation}
the structured increment is $c_j^\top B^\top$, and only the leftover
orthogonal to $\mathrm{col}(B)$ is mean-shifted from the probe set
$\mathcal{P}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mu_\perp
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{P}}
  \bigl(R_{j,:}-c_j^\top B^\top\bigr).
\end{equation}
Probes are overwritten with fresh values.
Tokens whose residual remains large relative to the probe scale are
fully recomputed~\citep{yao2024cacheblend}.

After RoPE, $\Delta K$ is not in $\mathrm{col}(B)$.
For keys we take the leading-$r$ right singular vectors of the probe
residual as the basis, at the same rank as the adapter.

\subsection{First-order analytic propagation}
\label{sec:analytic}

Given stored boundary activations $h_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $h_{\mathrm{mid}}$,
\dkv\ propagates $\Delta X$ with closed-form Jacobians
(Appendix~\ref{app:jac}):
the RMSNorm map, the softmax Jacobian
$a\odot(\delta-\langle a,\delta\rangle)$ inside attention, and the
SwiGLU product rule.
Two stabilizers limit compounding:
$\Delta X$ is rank-truncated after every layer, and reset to zero every
\texttt{hidden\_stride} layers (default $4$), after which that layer is
exact-patched from the snapshot.
The engine commits $\Delta W$ before propagating, so
$\mathrm{d}x\,{W'}^\top$ already contains the second-order
$\Delta X\Delta W$ term.

When $\Delta Q,\Delta K,\Delta V$ are rank-$r$, forming $\Delta S$ is
$O(n^2 r)$ rather than $O(n^2 d)$~\citep{dao2022flashattention}.
On short sequences a dense softmax Jacobian still dominates, which is
why the analytic route can be accurate without being cheaper than
prefill (\S\ref{sec:exp}).

\subsection{Spectral-condition hybrid routing}
\label{sec:hybrid}

Let $\kappa_\ell=\kappa(W_K^{(\ell)})\,\kappa(W_V^{(\ell)})$ with
$\kappa(W)=\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$, and
\begin{equation}
  \tilde\kappa_\ell \;=\; \prod_{j=\ell}^{L-1} \kappa_j.
\end{equation}
Shallow errors are amplified more.
A log-normalized $\tilde\kappa$ plus a depth prior yields a progressive
probe budget: up to $20\%$ of tokens in the first third of the stack and
in high-$\tilde\kappa$ layers, down to $5\%$ in deep, well-conditioned
layers.
Table~\ref{tab:plan} is the per-layer plan.
The default route is hybrid.
Probe and hybrid patches store the full patched-minus-base increment so
a later strict replay does not drop the zeroth-order stage.

\subsection{Error accounting and cost}
\label{sec:cost}

Zeroth-order error is a Lipschitz compound bound
$e_{\ell}=L_{\mathrm{lip}}\,e_{\ell-1}+\|\Delta W_\ell\|$
($L_{\mathrm{lip}}=1.2$ by default) so the lattice never under-reports.
Probe and analytic routes may replace the bound with a measured residual.
Chains add errors conservatively and rebase when
$\hat\varepsilon\ge 0.8\,\varepsilon$.

Approximate FLOPs are
\begin{align}
  c_{\mathrm{prefill}} &\approx L\bigl(nd^2 + n^2 d + nd\,d_{\mathrm{ff}}\bigr),\\
  c_{\mathrm{exact}}   &\approx (\#\text{LoRA maps})\cdot ndr,\\
  c_{\mathrm{probe}}   &\approx \rho\, c_{\mathrm{prefill}} + Lnd,\\
  c_{\mathrm{hybrid}}  &\approx c_{\mathrm{exact}} + \tfrac12 c_{\mathrm{probe}},\\
  c_{\mathrm{analytic}}&\approx L(ndr + n^2 r).
\end{align}
An automatic scheduler does not select a route whose billed FLOPs meet
or exceed re-prefill.

\section{Systems integration}
\label{sec:systems}

\dkv\ is a library.
The serving engines remain the serving engines.
Multi-adapter systems such as S-LoRA~\citep{sheng2024slora} and
Punica~\citep{chen2024punica} already batch heterogeneous LoRAs; they
still namespace or rebuild KV when the adapter set changes.
\dkv\ is complementary: it keeps one physical prefix and patches it.

\paragraph{vLLM.}
The V1 connector path loads an external module from
\texttt{kv\_connector\_module\_path}.
\texttt{DeltaKVConnector} implements \texttt{KVConnectorBase\_V1}.
On adapter load or RL weight broadcast it registers $\Delta W$ instead of
bumping a generation counter, materializes a patched view, and scatters
into allocated paged blocks
$[N_{\mathrm{blocks}}, B, n_{\mathrm{kv}}, d_h]$.
If $\varepsilon$ is exceeded, \texttt{get\_num\_new\_matched\_tokens}
returns $0$ and vLLM prefills.
The remaining engine-side change is to omit the adapter name from the
block hash when this connector is installed.

\paragraph{SGLang.}
Weight-version isolation stays off.
The \texttt{update\_weights\_*} RPCs run with
\texttt{flush\_cache=False}.
Weight version is a label on the delta, not a radix key.
In-flight requests keep private KV; the shared tree is patched in the
background.

\paragraph{LMCache and PEFT.}
Analytic propagation reads the existing hidden-state
store~\citep{lmcache}.
Chunk keys stay coupled to KV eviction.
PEFT state dictionaries~\citep{mangrulkar2022peft} of the form
\texttt{layers.$i$.self\_attn.k\_proj.lora\_A.weight} \emph{are} the
delta descriptor, with scaling $\alpha/r$.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We evaluate on the reference engine shipped with \dkv: a Llama-style
stack (RMSNorm, RoPE, grouped-query attention, SwiGLU) that is small
enough to run on CPU and complete enough that every algebraic claim is
testable.
This is a correctness validator, not a substitute for a 7B/70B serving
study (\S\ref{sec:limit}).
If the algebra is wrong, it fails here.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Prefill under $W_0$, commit a rank-$r$ LoRA, materialize a patched view,
and compare against a fresh prefill under $W_0+\Delta W$.
Metrics: max relative KV $\ell_2$, min cosine, next-token KL, log-prob
MAE, and the KIVI-4bit floor of the fresh cache.
Default configuration: $L{=}4$, $d{=}64$, $n_{\mathrm{heads}}{=}4$,
sequence length $64$, rank $4$, LoRA scale $10^{-2}$.
The run is deterministic given a fixed seed.

Table~\ref{tab:bench} supports four observations.
The adapter path on cached $X$ already lies inside 4-bit KV quantization
noise at $3.4\%$ of prefill.
Subspace/probe correction reduces relative $\ell_2$ from $0.0035$ to
$0.0021$ at $8$--$10\%$ of prefill.
Analytic matching is numerically exact on this stack, but the dense
softmax Jacobian makes it only a mild FLOP win at $n{=}64$, as the cost
model predicts.
Hybrid matches probe quality at lower billed FLOPs because boundary
layers stay exact.

\paragraph{Layer-$0$ exactness.}
For a $k$/$v$-only LoRA on layer~$0$, \eqref{eq:exact} matches a fresh
prefill to absolute error $<2{\times}10^{-5}$ and cosine $>0.9999$.

\paragraph{Subspace versus rank-1.}
On a residual $R=CB^\top+\mathbf{1}\mu_\perp^\top$ with varying codes
$C$, per-token least-squares onto $B$ recovers $\mathrm{col}(B)$ and
strictly beats a probe mean-shift.

\paragraph{ROME and the miss path.}
A rank-1 write into \texttt{down\_proj} of layer~$1$ patches without a
flush~\citep{meng2022rome}.
A LoRA of scale $1.0$, outside the patchable regime, is a miss.
After a hybrid materialize, a second lookup is strict and replays the
stored increment to $10^{-5}$.

Forty-eight unit tests cover RoPE linearity, the three Jacobians, lattice
decisions, PEFT parsing, adapter-swap subtraction, paged scatter, and
connector interfaces.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Prefix caches.}
PagedAttention~\citep{kwon2023vllm} and SGLang~\citep{zheng2024sglang}
made token-prefix reuse the default.
LMCache~\citep{lmcache} extends reuse off-device and stores hidden
states.
These systems still namespace or flush when $W$ moves.
\dkv\ reuses their storage; it changes the invalidation policy.

\paragraph{Multi-LoRA serving.}
S-LoRA~\citep{sheng2024slora} and Punica~\citep{chen2024punica} batch
many adapters efficiently.
They still treat adapter identity as part of the cache key.
\dkv\ keeps one physical prefix and patches across adapter swap.

\paragraph{Context-axis KV reuse.}
CacheBlend~\citep{yao2024cacheblend} recomputes tokens whose layer-1 KV
residual is large when stitching cached chunks.
Streaming and eviction methods (H2O, SnapKV) drop tokens under a
\emph{fixed} $W$.
\dkv\ reuses probe/gate machinery with a different trigger (weight
delta) and a different structure ($\mathrm{col}(B)$).

\paragraph{Low-rank weights and KV compression.}
LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora}, QLoRA~\citep{dettmers2023qlora}, and
PEFT~\citep{mangrulkar2022peft} made $\Delta W=sBA$ the production
adapter format; ROME and MEMIT~\citep{meng2022rome,meng2023memit} are
rank-1 edits.
KIVI~\citep{liu2024kivi} quantizes KV under fixed weights.
We use that 4-bit noise as an acceptance floor.

\paragraph{View maintenance.}
Materialized views are maintained under
$\Delta\mathrm{relation}$~\citep{gupta1993ivm}.
We maintain KV under $\Delta W$.
The lattice is the consistency contract.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limit}

The validator is a 4-layer, $d{=}64$ model.
It can falsify the algebra; it cannot measure tail latency on a 70B
deployment, nor error compounding through $32$--$80$ layers.
Paged GPU kernels for the adapter path and the one-line vLLM/SGLang
hooks remain engineering work.
The analytic route still densifies $\Delta S$ in PyTorch; a low-rank
attention kernel would change the flop ratio, not the Jacobian.
\dkv\ will not patch full supervised fine-tuning or an architecture
swap.
Mid-token in-flight decode under a weight update is out of scope: live
requests keep private KV; the shared store is patched in the background.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}

Serving engines already reuse KV along the token axis and invalidate it
along the weight axis.
For the $\Delta W$ that occur in production---LoRA swaps, rank-1 edits,
quantization-grid changes, KL-bounded RL steps---that invalidation is
unnecessary.
\dkv\ maintains the cache as a materialized view, applies an exact

\section{Jacobians}
\label{app:jac}

\paragraph{RMSNorm.}
Let $\mathrm{ms}=\mathrm{mean}(x^2)$, $\mathrm{rms}=\sqrt{\mathrm{ms}+\epsilon}$,
$y=(x/\mathrm{rms})\odot w$.
Then $\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{ms})=2\,\mathrm{mean}(x\odot\mathrm{d}x)$,
$\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{rms})=\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{ms})/(2\,\mathrm{rms})$, and
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{d}y
  \;=\;
  \frac{\mathrm{d}x\cdot\mathrm{rms}-x\cdot\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{rms})}{\mathrm{rms}^2}\odot w.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Softmax.}
For a row $a=\mathrm{softmax}(s)$,
$J_{\mathrm{softmax}}(a)=\mathrm{diag}(a)-aa^\top$, hence
$J_{\mathrm{softmax}}(a)[\delta]=a\odot(\delta-\langle a,\delta\rangle)$.

\paragraph{SwiGLU.}
$\mathrm{silu}(z)=z\,\sigma(z)$ and
$\mathrm{silu}'(z)=\sigma(z)+z\,\sigma(z)(1-\sigma(z))$.
The MLP differential is
$\mathrm{d}y=\bigl((\mathrm{silu}'(g)\odot\mathrm{d}g)\odot u
+\mathrm{silu}(g)\odot\mathrm{d}u\bigr)W_{\mathrm{down}}^\top$
plus the local adapter path.

\section{LoRA path after RoPE}
\label{app:lora}

PEFT computes $\Delta y=s\,(xA^\top)B^\top$.
Codes $c=s\,xA^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}$ and basis $B$ are the
compressed $\Delta K$/$\Delta V$ before RoPE.
After RoPE on $K$ the basis is position-dependent, so $\Delta K$ is
stored dense.
Compression relative to one KV layer is $d_{\mathrm{kv}}/r$ before RoPE
($64\times$ at $d_{\mathrm{kv}}=4096$, $r=64$).
Adapter swap concatenates $(A_B,-A_A)$ and $(B_B,B_A)$ without
materializing $\Delta W$.
